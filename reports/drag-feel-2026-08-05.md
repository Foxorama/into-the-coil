# The drag feels right, and both halves of "not quite" are the same number

**2026-08-05.** First play-test verdict on touch, and the first tuning pass on anything in this
project. Third use of the eyes-on instrument.

## The verdict

Played with a thumb, on a phone. Paraphrased from the report:

- It works **better than previous touch-screen games** the player has used.
- The drag has a **very slight delay**, and *"this is not the problem"* — it reads as engines firing,
  as turbo-lag, as the ship struggling against the environment. **Wanted: about 10% less.**
- A **large quick swipe keeps the ship flying** after the finger has left the glass. Also not a
  problem in itself. **Wanted: about 10% less.**
- Desktop has **none of that feel** — pure key movement, moves well, but does not feel like *flying*
  a ship.
- Deliberately asking for a small correction: *"I'd rather correct slowly than over-correct and have
  to spend ages trying to get it back to where it is now."*

## Both complaints are one mechanism

**The bank.** A finger routinely outruns the ship's top speed, so undelivered movement accumulates
and drains at `SHIP_SPEED`. That backlog *is* the delay during the drag, and the same pool is what
keeps draining after release. One quantity, felt in two places.

It also explains the desktop observation exactly: **a key asks for ±1 every step, never more than the
ship can deliver, so no bank ever forms.** The absence of the feel on desktop is not a missing
feature — it is the arithmetic.

⚠️ **The delay was never designed.** `docs/decisions/0032-touch-is-relative-drag-and-not-a-stick.md`
argues for the bank so that *a flick arrives in full rather than being clipped to one step*. The
turbo-lag is a side effect of that, discovered by a thumb. It is the best thing in the build and
nobody chose it.

## Measured, before and after

`844×390`, DPR 2, a fast 88px swipe over ~100ms, blit hook reading where the ship was actually drawn.
Reproducible to ±1.5px across runs.

| | 1.6 | **1.48** | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| ship travel after the finger lifts | 112.4px | **98.5px** | −12% |
| how long it keeps travelling | 300ms | **267ms** | −11% |
| total travel for the swipe | 140.8px | **130.2px** | −7.5% |

⚠️ **80% of a fast swipe's travel happens after the finger has gone**, at either value. That is the
scale of what is being tuned, and it is why the effect is so legible to a player.

### Why 1.48 and not 1.44

1.44 is the arithmetic −10% and it over-shot the thing that was actually asked for: **−15% on the
run-on**, because the drain during the swipe is fixed by `SHIP_SPEED`, so cutting demand leaves
proportionally less in the bank at release. 1.48 lands −11% and −12% on the two quantities named,
at half the disturbance to the swipe mapping. Chosen to honour *correct slowly*.

⚠️ **The first attempt was measured and discarded rather than shipped and re-felt.** That is the
whole value of having the instrument: two candidate tunings cost four minutes and no play-test.

## The knob not used, and why

**`SHIP_SPEED` is the better knob for this complaint** — raising it drains the bank faster, cutting
both the delay and the run-on while leaving the swipe mapping untouched. It was not used because it
is shared with the keyboard, and the keyboard is not what was played. Naming it here so the next pass
starts from the right menu rather than re-deriving it:

| knob | delay | run-on | swipe distance | other devices |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `DRAG_GAIN` ↓ | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ | untouched |
| `SHIP_SPEED` ↑ | ↓ | ↓ | unchanged | **keyboard and pad get faster** |
| a cap on bank depth | unchanged for small drags | ↓ hard | unchanged | untouched — but a big flick would stop arriving in full, which 0032 argues against |

## What this opens, and does not settle

**The feel belongs to the ship, not to the input device.** Right now a touch player flies something
with mass and a keyboard player flies something without, which is two different games from one
build — and 0032's own rule is that a control scheme is *a preference, never a difficulty setting*.

Giving every device the feel means putting inertia in `flyShip`, and `src/sim/flight.ts` currently
says the opposite in as many words: *"Response is immediate. No acceleration, no smoothing… That is
the arcade answer and it is a decision rather than an omission."*

**That is a decision to revisit, not a constant to nudge**, and it is not taken here. It would also
compound with the bank — touch would get inertia twice — so the touch numbers above would need
re-tuning downward as part of it, not before it.

⚠️ **The gamepad cannot be given "the same change".** A stick is a level, like a key: no bank, no
delay. It will feel like the keyboard and not like touch, and it waits on the same decision. Untested
so far — no pad connected.

### The pad was played, and it weakens the paragraph above

**Added 2026-08-05.** *"Confirmed gamepad on desktop works really well."*

The prediction that it *"will feel like the keyboard and not like touch"* was half right and drew the
wrong conclusion from it. The pad does have no bank — and it plays well anyway, with no tuning pass
and no second look.

So the variable separating the keyboard from the other two devices may not be **mass** at all. It may
be **continuity**: touch and the pad both ask for any value between 0 and 1, and a key can only ask
for nothing or everything.

| | bank | control |
|---|---|---|
| touch `drag` | yes | continuous |
| gamepad | no | continuous, analog |
| keyboard | no | three states — −1, 0, +1 |

If that is the answer, the fix is **a ramp on the keyboard axis alone** — no sim change, no reversal
of `src/sim/flight.ts`'s *"response is immediate"*, and no other device touched. Far cheaper than
inertia, and invisible until a third device had been played.

⚠️ **This changes step 2 of the ordering above from a certainty to a fork**, and the fork is settled
by a hand rather than by argument: does the pad feel *flown*, or merely precise? Until that is
answered, ship inertia is a candidate and not the plan.

### Answered the same day, and it was two questions

*"The gamepad feels more like flying because of the **diagonality** of the approach."*
*"The bank-lag or turbo-lag is **genuinely missing** — it's the diff between it feeling like flying
and it feeling like flying in a real environment."*

Both hypotheses hold. They separate different pairs:

| the difference between | is | which reads as |
|---|---|---|
| keyboard and gamepad | continuity — a stick pushes at any angle, a key at one of eight | *flying* |
| gamepad and touch | mass — the bank, the lag, the run-on | *flying in a real environment* |

**Step 2 is a certainty again**, and it grew a sibling. The revised order:

1. **`SHIP_SPEED`**, against real threats. Unchanged — everything below still calibrates off it.
2. **Ship inertia** — the decision. Confirmed, not a candidate. The turbo-lag is a property of the
   *ship*, which is what this project already says a control feel must be, and it currently reaches
   the player by accident on one device out of three.
3. **Keyboard continuity** — independent of 1 and 2, keyboard-only, and much cheaper: ramp the axis
   instead of snapping to ±1. It can be done at any point and blocks nothing.
4. **`DRAG_GAIN` and the bank last**, and now very likely *upward*: once the ship supplies the lag,
   touch would otherwise have it twice.

⚠️ **The good touch feel is not being taken away by this — it is being moved to where it belongs and
given to the other two devices.** That is worth stating plainly, because "we are removing the thing
you liked from the input layer" and "every device now feels like that" are the same change described
from two ends.

## Stopping here, and what re-opens it

**Verdict on 1.48: *"that feels really good."*** Tuning stops at one pass, deliberately, and the
reason is not time — it is that the quantities are not measurable yet.

⚠️ **The trigger is shared, and there is only one.** Three separate deferrals have accumulated and
they all wait on the same build:

| deferred | first raised | why it cannot be settled now |
|---|---|---|
| the scroll rate — *"slightly too fast"* | 2026-08-04 | lookahead is reaction time, and there is nothing to react to |
| `DRAG_GAIN`, and the run-on with it | this report | the run-on costs a player something only when there is something to hit |
| ship inertia for every device (below) | this report | it changes the touch numbers, so it must land before they are tuned again |

**The trigger: the first build with bullets and collisions.** Not a date. Until an entity can kill
the player, every one of these is a preference; after it, each is a difficulty measurement.

## The ordering that pass has to follow, or it gets done twice

⚠️ **`SHIP_SPEED` is the constant everything else calibrates against**, and it is the one this pass
deliberately did not touch. Bullet speed, enemy approach speed, the width of a dodge window and the
value of every assist ladder in `src/sim/assist.ts` are all *relative to how fast the player can get
out of the way*.

So when the pass comes:

1. **`SHIP_SPEED` first**, against real threats. Everything downstream depends on it.
2. **Ship inertia next** — the decision, not a constant. It changes what `SHIP_SPEED` feels like at
   the same value.
3. **`DRAG_GAIN` and the bank last**, because both of the above move them.

Doing it in any other order means tuning a number against a ceiling that then moves. That is the
predecessor's eight-pass bounce, arrived at from the other end — not measuring the wrong thing, but
measuring the right thing too early.

⚠️ **Nothing in the suite asserts on any of these values**, which is what keeps this cheap:
`docs/decisions/0027-measure-the-picture-not-the-model.md` refuses a guard on an unvalidated
threshold precisely so a later pass does not have to fight the tests to move one.

## The trigger has landed, and the ordering above is short by one

**Added 2026-08-05.** Bullets, contact and death are in —
`docs/decisions/0034-a-threat-is-absolute-and-a-pool-is-the-pairing.md`. An entity can kill the
player, so all three deferrals above are now measurable rather than preferences.

⚠️ **The scroll rate is listed as a deferral and is missing from the ordering.** It cannot be: it is
the *time* budget where `SHIP_SPEED` is the *distance* budget, and the dodge margin is a function of
both. 0034 has the argument and the correction — **step 1 is `SHIP_SPEED` and the scroll rate
together**, and `SCROLL_PER_STEP` has moved into `src/sim/flight.ts` beside `SHIP_SPEED` so they can
be turned in one place. Steps 2 and 3 are unchanged.

⚠️ **And one sentence above is a trap for whoever runs that pass.** *"Bullet speed, enemy approach
speed … are all relative to how fast the player can get out of the way"* is a statement about the
ORDER, not an instruction to author them as ratios — written as ratios the dodge margin is invariant
under `SHIP_SPEED` and step 1 measures nothing. 0034 makes that a guard rather than a warning.

## Step 1 is done, and the answer was no change

**Added 2026-08-05.** `reports/ship-speed-settled-2026-08-05.md`. Played against real threats:
*"very easy to dodge and fly around, but requires active choice and concentration… a good starting
difficulty to build on."*

**`SHIP_SPEED` stays at 1.7 and `SCROLL_PER_STEP` at 0.6**, now with a hand behind them rather than a
genre guess. Step 2 (ship inertia) and step 3 (`DRAG_GAIN`) are released, and level content is safe
to author against constants that will not move.

⚠️ The same session found that **every hit was contact and no enemy shot ever landed** — the dodge
has ~30× the room it needs. So the bullet half of the threat model was bypassed rather than
validated, and `spit`'s speed and the lancer's fire rate are still unfelt. That is a content-layer
question and it does not re-open anything here.
