# 0396 — The fish has a tail

**Accepted 2026-09-27.** The flying fish's caudal fin is two broad blades and a V-shaped fork — a fin
you can see is forked at every angle the beat turns it through — rather than two slivers.

## The ask

> the tail looks... I don't even know, but it's not great.

## What the picture showed

The sheet showed a **boomerang**: [0374](0374-the-fish-beats-its-tail.md)'s lobes were 0.15 of the
tile's radius across at their widest, swept forward from the fork, with tips only 0.48 aft of the
root. In play, turned through a leap, the one lobe edge-on read as a **stick** poking out of the body.
A first redraw that only lengthened the lobes photographed as a crescent — still not a fin.

## What changed

Each lobe is a blade about a third of the radius across at mid-length — 0.48 where the old one was
0.15 — its leading edge running straight out from the peduncle, and the fork cut as a V back to 0.5.
Four rays fan into each blade from the root, each ending short of both edges. The grown body's tail is
the same shape drawn out a little further. The base under the body is where
[0381](0381-the-fish-is-bigger.md) put it, so the join is still under the flesh. The streamer that
left each lobe tip was taken off with the wings' — [0395](0395-the-fish-wears-its-fire.md).

## The guards

None new. The shape is a picture question and was answered by photographing it — on the sheet at 2×
and in the fight at each stage. 0374's and 0381's guards on the tail's root, beat, layer and join pass
unmoved; 0381's probe for *the fin's base pulled back behind its root* was re-anchored on the new points
and breaks the same thing.
